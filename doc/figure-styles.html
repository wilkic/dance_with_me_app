Four ways to draw a dancer

The same dancer, drawn by the app's own code, in each style. In every picture the big purple one is you, the green one is a guest behind you, and the small yellow one is the size of the DJ at the booth.

What each one is

  • Stick is what's live today.
  • Hands + feet is the same figure with a hand and a foot added as bones, exactly as thick as the limbs. A hand points where the fingers are; a foot runs from heel to toe.
  • Hands + shoulders adds real shoulders: the arms hang off the shoulder points, rounding over from the neck, instead of off the neck itself.
  • Curved is a filled silhouette: a thick torso, arms that bend in a curve and taper to the wrist, tapered legs with a straight thigh and shin, small feet, and a head floating clear of the shoulders.

A spine that bends

The camera's pose engine gives no points between the shoulders and the hips, so a "core" or "chest" point can't be measured. It can be worked out: the spine leaves the neck square to the shoulder line and reaches the pelvis square to the hip line, and when the two tilt differently the curve between them is the bend. Spine: bends draws that curve. Its middle is the core point and a quarter of the way down is the chest. It shows side bends and hip sways; bending toward or away from the camera stays invisible.

Try them on yourself

On branch figure-styles, the dance screen has a fourth button at the bottom, Figure, that steps through the four. The bending spine is on this page only for now. It applies to you and to everyone in your space, and is remembered on the phone.

The dancer on this page is a made-up puppet, so its hands and feet are always exactly where they should be. On a real camera the finger and toe points are the least steady ones, so the phone test is what counts for hands and feet.

The drawing code on this page is the app's (src/avatar/stickFigure.js), bundled in. Rebuild with doc/figure-styles.build.sh.
